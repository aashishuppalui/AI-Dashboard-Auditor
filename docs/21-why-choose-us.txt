01 — We don't just critique the interface
Others

"This chart is unclear."

Us

"This chart makes it difficult to determine whether delivery performance is improving or deteriorating."

Difference:

We connect UX problems to the user's decision.

02 — Every important conclusion has evidence
Others

Finding
↓
Recommendation

Us

Evidence
↓
Finding
↓
Recommendation

The user can trace:

Why did the AI say this?

That's critical for professional UX work.

03 — We prioritize instead of overwhelming

ChatGPT's response gives a large number of potential improvements.

That's useful during brainstorming.

But a product team needs:

What should we do first?

Our model:

P0 → P1 → P2

And eventually we can make this even better:

Highest impact → Supporting improvements → Optimization

The product should reduce decision burden, not increase it.

04 — We separate observation from interpretation

This is subtle but powerful.

Observation

"The trend chart contains a 95% target line but no visible actual data series."

Interpretation

"Users cannot assess whether performance has improved or declined over time."

Recommendation

"Restore the actual trend series and provide current-period context."

This separation increases trust.

05 — We don't invent business logic

This could become one of our strongest principles.

If the UI doesn't tell us:

"Suppliers below 90% are considered at risk"

we shouldn't invent that.

Instead:

"Allow users to filter suppliers by performance."

This makes the tool safer for enterprise use.

07 — We maintain consistency

This one is easy to underestimate.

We have already discovered this ourselves.

Same screenshot shouldn't produce:

"The biggest issue is navigation"

then five minutes later:

"The biggest issue is KPI hierarchy"

then:

"The biggest issue is the trend chart."

A professional reviewer needs a stable conclusion.

Our principle:

Same evidence → same underlying conclusion.

That's a legitimate product-quality differentiator.

After seeing this ChatGPT response, I'd change our competitive map.

Competitor A

15. Our first draft of the "Why Us?" statement

I'd put this into our V2 strategy document:

Why would someone choose UX Review Companion?

Because it doesn't just tell teams what looks wrong. It helps them understand what matters, why it matters, and what to improve first.

UX Review Companion combines interface understanding, observable evidence, decision-focused reasoning, prioritization and actionable recommendations into one repeatable review process.

Evidence before opinion.
Decisions before aesthetics.
Actions before endless critique.

And then four supporting pillars:

1. Decision-aware

Reviews connect UX issues to the user's actual decision or task.

2. Evidence-backed

Important findings can be traced back to observable interface evidence.

3. Prioritized

The tool tells teams what deserves attention first instead of producing an unranked list of UX issues.

4. Repeatable

The same review framework can be applied consistently across screenshots, Figma designs and live products.
